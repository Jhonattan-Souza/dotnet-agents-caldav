## Before

The Occurrence adapter decoded a cursor into the original query and last semantic key, called `ICalendarService.QueryOccurrencesAsync` again, filtered everything before that key, and constructed the next page and cursor locally. Every continuation could therefore repeat discovery, candidate REPORT, resource retrieval, parsing, recurrence expansion, ordering, and projection. The host filter and the adapter also owned independent 30-second deadlines. Resource href was absent from the cursor comparison, so otherwise equal effective-start, Calendar, UID, and Recurrence Identity keys did not have a complete traversal tie-breaker.

The focused baseline observation at `e63ea4d62fa4b4062566a6819127c18a30a1a38d` uses 201 stable distinct-UID Occurrences at page sizes 1, 50, and 200. Observation-only fixture `CalendarOccurrenceToolsTests.BaselineContinuationReexecutesTheComplete201OccurrenceQuery` recorded 201, five, and two service-query invocations at the real legacy adapter seam, with respective Passed TRX durations of 77 ms, 156 ms, and 979 ms. The service itself is a deterministic substitute returning the fixed prebuilt corpus; the legacy adapter source shows that each invocation would re-enter the complete upstream query. Distinct UIDs are required for successful legacy traversal because its cursor comparison omitted Resource href; the changed equal-key correctness corpus therefore cannot be presented as an identical baseline input. This observation proves repeated adapter-to-service dispatch for a traversable 201-row legacy corpus, not the duration of discovery, retrieval, parsing, or recurrence expansion.
